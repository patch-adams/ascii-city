Registration

twelve frames · four inks · none of them agree
wash · paint halftone · press ascii · text contour · drawing

Four descriptions of the same man, printed on top of each other and deliberately out of register — the way a badly aligned press job separates into its plates.

None of the layers is the picture. The wash is him as paint, the dot screen is him as press, the character field is him as text, and the hairlines are him as drawing. They all carry the same frame and they all sit a few millimetres apart, so the image and its own description can't quite line up.

Everything outside him stays paper. That's the part that makes it a print instead of a filter — the type has a silhouette, not a rectangle. There's no segmentation model here: the footage has shallow depth of field, so local sharpness finds the subject and the room falls away on its own.

A faint ghost of the previous frame's drawing sits under each one, so a still still carries the motion.
